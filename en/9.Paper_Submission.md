# Paper Submission

After completing the paper, submission is the critical step to bring your research to the academic community. Submission is not just clicking a "Submit" button—it is a systematic process that includes venue selection, format checking, the submission workflow, handling reviews, and subsequent revisions. This chapter covers the key points of this process.

## Pre-Submission Preparation

Before formally submitting, make sure to complete the following checks:

### Format and Content Self-Check

- **Template and Format**: Ensure you are using the official LaTeX or Word template provided by the target conference/journal. Page count, fonts, line spacing, margins, etc. must all conform to requirements. Many submissions are desk-rejected due to formatting issues, which is a waste.
- **Anonymization (Double-Blind Review)**: Most CS conferences use double-blind review, meaning the paper must not contain any information that could reveal the authors' identities, including: author names and affiliations, acknowledgments, code repository links (if containing personal info), and overly obvious self-citations (e.g., "in our previous work [1]" should be changed to "in [1]").
- **Reference Check**: Confirm that all citations are consistent in format and complete (year, venue, page numbers, etc.). Replace arXiv preprints with published versions when available. Using DBLP for standard BibTeX entries is a good practice.
- **Supplementary Material (Appendix)**: If the conference supports appendices or supplementary materials, you can include additional experimental details, proofs, and extra figures there, with cross-references in the main text.
- **Grammar and Spelling**: Run one final pass through Grammarly or a similar tool to catch any remaining errors.

### Co-Author Confirmation

Before submission, confirm with all co-authors: the paper content, author ordering, corresponding author, and target venue. This is an important part of academic integrity and helps avoid unnecessary disputes later.

## Venue Selection Strategy

Choosing the right target venue requires experience and judgment. Common considerations include:

- **Relevance**: Does the paper's topic fall within the target venue's Call for Papers / Scope? This is the most basic criterion. Submitting to an irrelevant venue almost guarantees rejection.
- **Ranking and Impact**: Refer to ranking systems such as the CCF Recommended List, JCR Quartiles, and CAS Classifications (see Chapter 1 for details), and match your paper's quality and innovation level to the highest reachable tier.
- **Review Cycle**: Conferences have fixed deadlines with relatively predictable review cycles (typically 2-4 months). Journal review cycles vary widely—some may take six months or longer. Plan ahead if you have graduation deadlines.
- **Acceptance Rate and Competition**: Top conferences typically have acceptance rates around 20-25%. Knowing the historical acceptance rate helps you assess the difficulty.
- **Deadline Calendar**: Use tools like [CCFDDL](https://ccfddl.com/), [MyHuiban](https://www.myhuiban.com/), and [call4papers](https://www.call4papers.cn/) to track submission deadlines and plan your writing schedule.

Note: Discuss your target venue thoroughly with your advisor. Their experience and field knowledge can help you make a more informed choice.

## Submission Systems and Workflow

Common submission systems in computer science include:

- **HotCRP**: Used by many top security and systems conferences (e.g., CCS, USENIX Security, S&P).
- **OpenReview**: Widely used in AI (e.g., ICLR, NeurIPS, ICML), with publicly visible reviews.
- **CMT (Conference Management Toolkit)**: Microsoft's submission system, used by some conferences.
- **EasyChair**: A widely used conference management system.
- **Journal Portals**: IEEE, ACM, Elsevier, Springer, etc. each have their own submission platforms.

Key points during submission:

- **Register Early**: Don't wait until the deadline to create an account. Some systems require an earlier Abstract Submission Deadline before the Full Paper Deadline.
- **Fill in Submission Details**: Title, abstract, keywords, author list, and Conflict of Interest declarations—fill these out accurately and honestly.
- **Upload the Final Version**: Check file size limits and ensure the PDF renders correctly (different systems may display PDFs differently). Preview on multiple devices before submitting.
- **Time Zones**: Deadlines are usually in AoE (Anywhere on Earth, UTC-12). Don't miss the deadline due to time zone miscalculations. Submit at least a few hours early, as systems may experience congestion near the deadline.

## Review and Rebuttal

### The Review Process

After submission, the paper goes through the following review process (conference example):

1. **Desk Review**: The PC Chair or Area Chair checks whether the paper meets basic requirements (format, topic relevance, anonymization). Papers that fail are immediately rejected.
2. **Reviewer Assignment**: Typically 3-4 reviewers are assigned to each paper.
3. **Review Phase**: Reviewers evaluate the paper on dimensions such as novelty, technical correctness, experimental thoroughness, and writing quality, providing scores and detailed comments.
4. **Discussion Phase**: Reviewers and the Area Chair discuss to reach a preliminary consensus.
5. **Author Response (Rebuttal)**: Some venues allow authors to respond to reviews before the final decision.
6. **Final Decision**: Accept / Reject / Revise (journals typically also have Major Revision and Minor Revision).

### Rebuttal Strategies

The rebuttal is an important opportunity to influence the final outcome. Some tips:

- **Address Every Point**: Respond to each reviewer's comment individually. Organize by reviewer, numbered point by point.
- **Be Respectful**: Thank reviewers for valid criticisms and explain your plan for improvement. For misunderstandings, clarify politely with evidence. Avoid confrontational language.
- **Use Evidence**: If reviewers question experimental results, provide additional data or analysis to support your claims. Verbal defense alone is rarely convincing.
- **Prioritize**: Focus first on critical issues that affect the acceptance decision (e.g., novelty concerns, technical correctness). Minor formatting or wording issues can be addressed briefly.
- **Length Limits**: Rebuttals usually have word or page limits. Communicate the most critical information within the allotted space.
- **Team Effort**: Writing the rebuttal should involve all co-authors and your advisor for collective input.

## Handling Submission Outcomes

### Paper Accepted

- **Camera-Ready Preparation**: Submit the final version within the deadline, incorporating reviewer feedback and removing anonymization (adding author names, acknowledgments, etc.). Camera-ready deadlines are usually tight.
- **Copyright Agreement**: Sign the copyright transfer form as required by the venue.
- **Registration and Presentation**: Most conferences require at least one author to register and present the paper (Oral Presentation or Poster Session). For guidance on preparing academic presentations, see ["Basic Literacy in Graphics and Vision Research"](https://pengsida.net/games003/), specifically the section on "Academic Presentation Preparation."

### Paper Rejected

Rejection is a normal part of research—don't be overly discouraged. The key is to learn and improve from the reviews:

- **Carefully Analyze the Reviews**: Reviewers' comments usually point out real weaknesses. Even if some comments seem unfair, calmly consider whether there is room for improvement.
- **Revise and Resubmit**: Make targeted revisions based on the reviews and submit to another suitable venue. Do not resubmit an unrevised paper—it is both unethical and a wasted opportunity.
- **Discuss with Your Advisor**: Analyze the reasons for rejection together and plan the next steps.

### Journal Revision (Major/Minor)

A distinctive feature of journal submissions is the revision opportunity:

- **Response Letter**: Write a detailed response letter addressing each reviewer comment and explaining the changes made.
- **Highlight Changes**: Use tools like latexdiff to generate a tracked-changes version for reviewers.
- **Mind the Deadline**: Major Revisions typically allow 2-3 months; Minor Revisions allow about 1 month. Submit within the deadline.

## Academic Integrity Reminders

- **Dual Submission**: Do not submit the same paper to multiple venues simultaneously (unless explicitly permitted, e.g., with a clear extension requirement between a workshop and a full version). This is a serious violation of academic integrity.
- **Proper Citation**: Honestly cite related work, don't omit key references, and don't overstate your contributions.
- **Data and Result Integrity**: Experimental data and results must be truthful and reliable—never fabricate or manipulate them.
